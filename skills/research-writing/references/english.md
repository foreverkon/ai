# English manuscript realization

Use when filling or reviewing English prose. Discussion and intentions may remain Chinese. For fluid-mechanics structure, terminology, quantitative claims and figure language, also read [fluid writing](domains/fluid-writing.md).

- Use one canonical term for one concept. Prefer field usage verified in supplied literature over attractive synonyms. Keep different phenomena distinct even if a generic verb could describe both.
- Define abbreviations as full name (abbreviation) at first use, separately in abstract and body where needed. Reuse symbols, units, normalization, and sign conventions.
- Put familiar context before new concepts. Introduce apparatus, notation and coined labels before relying on them.
- Give each sentence its planned job. Use connective words for the actual logical relation: contrast, cause, consequence or addition.
- Keep qualifiers that alter scientific meaning. Delete qualifiers only when removing them preserves the claim and its boundary; qualifiers such as `time-averaged`, `linear`, and `finite-amplitude` can be essential technical distinctions.
- Match verb strength to the evidence for that claim; retain uncertainty where it affects meaning.
- Remove repetition and empty claims while retaining necessary conditions, comparisons and definitions.

For a paragraph realization pass: read its intention and ordered sentence leaves → inspect evidence → compose connected sentences → reverse-outline the prose → check each realized act against the planned leaf. Add a leaf when the argument needs another sentence intention. Grammar and idiom corrections preserving intention do not need renewed intent approval.
